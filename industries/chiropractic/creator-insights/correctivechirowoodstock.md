## Patterns

### Hook Preferences

- Opens with community framing: "Meet...", "I love supporting...", partner shoutouts
- Educational hooks are present but weakly executed: "When is the best time to get a chiropractic adjustment?" is a strong question but delivered as a wall-of-text photo post, killing reach
- No pattern interrupts, no spoken hooks, no kinetic text visible in top posts
- Collab content outperforms solo posts 5-10x consistently
- The one standout exception: HoneyBee Salon's partner video (posted FROM the salon account) hit 1,340 views and 40 likes, the second-best performer, and it was essentially a patient story / office tour delivered by a third party

### Format Preferences

- Carousels dominate the post count but underperform on views/likes vs. videos
- Videos (Reels) are the only format generating 400+ views
- Static educational posts (photos) average 1-8 likes regardless of topic
- No evidence of scripted voiceover Reels with text overlays
- No Remotion-style motion graphics or animated explainers
- Community event carousels (Bingo Night, Halloween office costume) generate moderate engagement (13-27 likes) and appear to serve retention rather than acquisition

### Posting Cadence

- Instagram: roughly 2-4 posts per week based on post density; holiday/seasonal posts cluster in Q4
- TikTok: ~12 total videos, no observable cadence, appears sporadic or test phase
- Content themes by month: Q4 holiday push (Nov-Dec), New Year reset offers (Jan), Valentine's giveaway (Feb), spring transition (late Feb), elbow exercise video (most recent substantive content)
- No consistent series or recurring content format identified

## Strengths (Learn From)

1. **Community networking works.** Their top posts all involve real local businesses and real people. The HoneyBee Salon collab is their best content and it was made by the partner, not them. Cross-pollination of audiences drives their only meaningful reach.
2. **Patient transformation reveal has legs.** The HoneyBee video worked because it featured a real patient discovering scoliosis during their exam. That narrative arc (unknown problem revealed by technology) is compelling and maps directly to their digital posture/thermal scan differentiator.
3. **Events drive community retention.** Bingo nights, push-up challenges, and holiday carousels create patient stickiness even at small scale. This is a real community.
4. **Dr. Andrew is visible and personable.** His marathon completion post, his participating in the push-up challenge, and his on-camera collab videos show he is willing to be on camera and has authentic personality.

---

## Gaps (What They're NOT Doing — Our Opportunity)

1. **Zero technology content.** Their entire differentiator is Digital Posture Analysis + Thermal Scans, and they have posted essentially nothing about it. No "what a thermal scan actually shows" Reel, no posture before/after, no dramatic scan reveal. This is a massive unclaimed content territory.
2. **No educational video series.** 632 posts and no recognizable content format or series. No "Myth Monday," no "Ask the Doc," no recurring explainer. Every post is one-off.
3. **No scripted short-form Reels with hooks.** All their video content is casual, unscripted, community-style. There is no evidence of a single hook-driven educational Reel with proper caption structure, text overlays, or a clear CTA.
4. **No condition-specific content.** One tennis/golfer's elbow video in 30+ posts. No neck pain, no headaches, no sciatica, no posture content despite posture being their primary service differentiator.
5. **No patient testimonials filmed properly.** The HoneyBee video was made by the partner account and is their best content. They have not replicated this formula once.
6. **TikTok completely underdeveloped.** 12 videos total with no consistent handle in search. They have not made a TikTok bet at all.
7. **Low-frequency posting relative to post count.** 632 posts but only ~1,500 followers suggests they have been posting for years without a growth strategy. Volume without hooks does not compound.
8. **No family or pediatric content.** Despite serving families and athletes, nothing visible targets parents, pregnancy, pediatric chiropractic, or school-age kids.

## Competitive Positioning Summary

Corrective Chiropractic Woodstock has a real community, a strong doctor, and a clinically compelling differentiator (tech-forward diagnostics) that they are almost entirely failing to communicate through content. Their social presence reads like a neighborhood bulletin board, not a clinical authority. The gap between their diagnostic tools and their content execution is our largest opportunity: own the "evidence-based, diagnostic-first" content territory before they figure out it exists. Produce the digital posture and thermal scan content they should be making. Their community-first approach is a genuine strength in retention, not acquisition. We can win on acquisition with educational Reels while respecting that local community authenticity matters.
