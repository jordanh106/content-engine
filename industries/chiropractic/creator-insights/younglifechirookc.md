## Overview



@younglifechirookc is the Instagram presence of an OKC pediatric and prenatal chiropractic practice, with 7,397 followers and 2,832 posts, billing itself as "Helping OKC families thrive with gentle, expert chiropractic care" and highlighting Perinatal and Webster Certifications in the bio.

 The practice is led by 

Dr. Alasha Young, who received her Doctorate in Chiropractic from Parker University in 2017 and holds certifications in the Webster Prenatal Technique, EPIC Pediatric certification, BIRTHFIT, and Spinning Babies, and is an active member of the International Chiropractic Pediatric Association.

 The team has grown beyond Dr. Alasha: 

Dr. Tatum, born and raised in Edmond, Oklahoma, earned her Doctor of Chiropractic and a master's in Strength and Human Performance from Parker University, and is passionate about educating and empowering families, especially moms and babies, through nervous system-focused care.





Young Life Chiropractic is an OKC office specializing in family health, prenatal, and pediatric care, with a mission to help the OKC community not just survive but thrive, and a vision of "a thriving authentic community that trusts the power within their bodies."

 Content pillars directly mirror the practice's clinical specialties: 

the practice provides gentle, nervous-system-focused spinal care for expectant parents, infants, and families, blending Webster prenatal technique, craniosacral therapy, and family-centered adjustments, along with myofascial unwinding, biofield therapies, perinatal manual care, and functional oral tie protocols.



The account's 2,832 posts over its life suggest a highly active, consistent content output, though specific per-post engagement metrics are not publicly surfaced in search results. 

The Facebook page has 2,386 likes and 17 people currently talking about it, serving as a secondary distribution channel.

 The account does not appear to have a confirmed TikTok presence. Content is Instagram-native, with Facebook cross-posting evident, including reels demonstrating specific techniques like the Forward Leaning Inversion.

## Key Takeaways

- **Mirror their nervous-system content framework.** Their educational language around the brain-body connection, nervous system regulation, and spinal communication resonates with their audience. We can adopt the same framework while adding our own voice and depth.
- **Webster Certification is a top trust signal for prenatal content.** 

Their positioning of Webster as "a safe and gentle chiropractic technique that releases intrauterine constraint" and helping "the baby get into the best position possible"

 is exactly the language prenatal audiences are already searching for. We should own this same language in our own content.
- **Pediatric-specific condition content drives high-intent traffic.** Their ADHD, sleep, digestion, and behavioral health content pages signal that condition-specific pediatric content (not just "kids get adjusted too") earns engagement from parents actively searching for answers.
- **A multi-doctor team is a content asset.** 

Dr. Tatum's passion for "educating and empowering families, especially moms and babies" and her belief in "the body's innate ability to heal when given the right support"

 shows how practitioner-specific content expands the content calendar without repeating the same face.
- **2,832 posts at 7.4K followers = volume-first strategy, not virality-first.** This is a community-building and trust-building model. We should use this as a benchmark signal: consistency beats viral swings at this growth stage.
- **Facebook cross-posting is table stakes.** They actively cross-post to Facebook, which matters for their OKC family audience (parents, grandparents). We should ensure every Reel is cross-posted.
- **Oral tie and craniosacral content is an underexploited niche.** 

Dr. Alasha's advanced training in "functional oral tie protocols" and "craniosacral therapy"

 represents a specific, passionate parent community (tongue-tie, lip-tie, breastfeeding issues) that we can also target with dedicated content.
- **Postpartum physical demand content converts.** 

Content about postpartum challenges like "holding that baby, breastfeeding, hormonal changes" and the body's difficulty "normalizing bio-mechanics and function" due to relaxin

 speaks to a highly motivated patient segment ready to book.
- **Drug-free and proactive messaging is their differentiator and ours.** 

Framing around "parents choosing to be proactive with their children's health, rather than reactive" and chiropractic as "both proactive and preventive when it comes to wellness"

 is messaging we should also lead with.
- **Small account, high post count = strong local trust signal.** Their model demonstrates that for local family practices, volume, consistency, and niche depth build more trust than chasing follower counts.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **No confirmed TikTok presence.** Young Life Chiropractic appears to be Instagram and Facebook only. A strong TikTok strategy targeting the same prenatal and pediatric audience is an immediate differentiator.
- **No humor or personality layer visible.** Their content appears earnest and educational but lacks the deadpan, personality-forward voice that makes content shareable beyond their existing audience. Jordan's natural humor style is a real competitive edge here.
- **No athletic adult or teen content confirmed.** Their positioning is prenatal, pediatric, and family but does not appear to extend to athletes, teen athletes, or active adults. This is a full audience segment we can own.
- **No senior/grandparent content.** 

Their own Facebook bio says "we serve all stages and ages of life,"

 but senior-specific content is not evident. Grandparents who bring grandchildren in are an underserved content audience.
- **No "what to expect at your first visit" content confirmed.** 

Their website mentions "a friendly intake, a nervous system-focused assessment, and an explanation of recommended hands-on approaches,"

 but a walkthrough video format (Format E) targeting new patients considering their first visit is a gap they appear not to have filled with video.
- **No desk worker or adult daily life content.** Their content is almost entirely pregnancy, pediatric, and family wellness. Everyday adult content (text neck, desk posture, stress and the spine) is a wide-open lane.
- **No exercise or at-home care demo content confirmed.** Beyond the Forward Leaning Inversion demo, there is no confirmed library of at-home stretches, movements, or exercises for any audience segment. Format C content with frequency/difficulty guidance is a gap.
- **No myth-busting content confirmed at volume.** Given the skepticism many parents have about infant and pediatric chiropractic, a consistent Format D (Myth Buster) series is missing and highly needed in this niche.
