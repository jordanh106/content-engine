## Overview



Dr. Josh Adams is a Sports Medicine Chiropractor in St. Louis, Missouri, and the CEO and majority partner at P.R.I.M.E. Health Centers, a multi-location practice in the St. Louis area.

 

By his own description, he is "not your typical chiropractor," who loves treating tough cases where others have failed and working with medical doctors on staff to deliver a truly integrated approach to healthcare.

 His self-styled nickname is "The Peoples Doc," and his content brand is "CRACKED," a word that does double duty as chiropractic descriptor and personality statement.



Dr. Adams uses science to drive treatment rather than philosophy, and received a master's in sports rehabilitation, utilizing a full-body approach that includes spinal adjustments along with all other joints.

 

He has served as a chiropractic consultant for organizations including the St. Louis Rams, Gateway PGA, Missouri Baptist University, and the University of Missouri, and has provided care for professional athletes and high-profile celebrities across Florida, California, Texas, New Jersey, and New York.

 This elite athlete access gives his content a credibility hook that most chiropractic creators cannot match.



Dr. Josh is one of the few chiropractic doctors who share informative chiropractic content while actively engaging with his followers on social media, and also gives viewers a sneak peek into his personal life.

 His TikTok page shows a healthy mix of viral-scale videos (pinned videos with 28.5M, 2.1M, and 12.4M views) alongside consistent mid-tier content ranging from 20K to 150K+ views, confirming an audience built on entertainment value as much as clinical credibility.

## Key Takeaways

- **Brand the word, not just the practice.** "CRACKED" is a single-word brand identity that is ownable, visual, and instantly communicates chiropractic without saying chiropractor. We should audit whether "Collective" or a subset phrase can function the same way across our content.

- **Elite patients are content currency.** 

His history treating the St. Louis Rams, PGA players, and college athletic programs

 provides a credibility anchor that makes everyday content more believable. We should feature any athletes, coaches, or community figures we treat (with consent).

- **DIY + disclaimer is a repeatable format.** The "Thumb crack tutorial + read disclaimer at end" structure is a smart tension device. We can replicate this with prenatal stretches, infant tummy time adjustments, or pediatric home exercises, always with the professional guardrail built in.

- **Giveaway mechanics spike comment counts.** The SpineDeck giveaway drove 11.4K comments vs. a normal baseline of dozens. We can replicate this for our own product partners (pillows, foam rollers, baby carriers) or free visit giveaways.

- **Pinned video strategy controls first impressions.** His three pinned videos (28.5M, 12.4M, 2.1M) are carefully selected to maximize new follower conversion. We should pin our single best explainer, best demo, and best patient story.

- **Reply-to-comment content removes creative pressure.** His "Reply to @angie_spamz What's Next?" format uses audience questions as a content calendar. We should actively solicit questions in comments and turn the best ones into reply videos.

- **Sports-specific tagging expands discovery.** Tagging #soccer #football #runner #athlete on relevant content pulls in sport-specific audiences who are not yet chiropractic-aware. We can do the same for youth sports, youth athletes, postpartum fitness, and prenatal yoga communities.

- **ASMR/satisfying content is a separate algorithm lever.** His short, sound-led adjustment clips with #asmr tagging reach an audience that was never searching for chiropractic at all. A well-captured adjustment sound is its own hook.

- **Integrated medical positioning builds authority.** 

His emphasis on working with medical doctors on staff gives him a "best of both worlds" authority frame.

 We should explicitly communicate our collaborative or referral relationships with OBs, pediatricians, and sports medicine MDs.

- **Personal life content is a retention tool.** 

Dr. Josh actively engages with his followers and shares personal life moments alongside clinical content,

 which builds parasocial warmth. Jordan should use personal glimpses (family, behind-the-scenes, outside-the-office moments) to deepen audience loyalty.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **Zero pediatric and prenatal content.** His entire positioning is sports medicine and adult pain. There is no evidence of infant adjustments, prenatal chiropractic, pediatric content, or family-first messaging. This is our primary differentiator and his largest blind spot.

- **No family unit framing.** He treats individuals, not families. Content showing mom, dad, kids, and grandparents all receiving care in the same practice visit is a storytelling angle he never touches.

- **No postpartum recovery content.** His sports rehab focus skips the postpartum population entirely, which is one of the highest-need and highest-search demographics for chiropractic.

- **No infant/newborn adjustment education.** Pediatric chiropractic is one of the most searched and most misunderstood topics in our niche. His audience has never seen a gentle newborn adjustment explained with warmth and science. Ours can.

- **No myth-busting specific to pediatric safety.** "Is chiropractic safe for babies?" is a search-driven question with enormous traffic. His content does not address it. Ours should own this Format D space.

- **Education-first tone is underdeveloped.** His content leans heavily on spectacle and entertainment. There is limited deep-dive explainer content about why adjustments work, what causes dysfunction, or how nervous system health connects to overall wellness. Our 70% educational tone is a contrast brand position.

- **No senior/aging population content.** His demographic skews young and athletic. Content addressing seniors, healthy aging, balance, and longevity is entirely absent from his feed and represents a reachable audience.

- **Collaborative family content (treat the whole room) is unoccupied.** A video format where the whole family gets adjusted in one visit, narrated as a shared experience, does not exist in his library or in the broader chiro TikTok space to any significant degree.
