100% Chiropractic Woodstock (@100chiropractic_woodstock) has 897 followers, 260 following, and 734 posts on Instagram, with a bio positioning them around "exceptional corrective chiropractic care" and name-dropping two doctors: Dr. Alban Sims and Dr. Cal Criswell.

, 

with roots going back to a Colorado Springs practice founded in 2004.

 The Woodstock location operates as a franchise unit, which means content is almost certainly driven by corporate templates rather than local doctor personality.

A TikTok account (@100chirowoodstock) also exists, with at least one video using franchise-standard hashtags like #atlantachiropractic and #100percentchiropractic. No original educational content, no doctors on camera with personality, and no pediatric or prenatal content was found anywhere in their digital footprint. This account is a textbook case study in what not to do: high post frequency, franchise-dictated creative, zero local human connection, and a community that has tuned them out completely.

## Key Takeaways

- **High post count, near-zero growth is a warning, not a benchmark.** 734 posts producing 897 followers is proof that showing up consistently with bad content is worse than doing nothing. Our quality-first approach is the correct counterstrategy.
- **No doctor on camera = no trust.** The most glaring gap across every platform is the absence of a real human face and voice. Jordan on camera is a structural competitive advantage in this market, not a nice-to-have.
- **The franchise voice is the weakness.** Corporate template content reads as generic because it is. Our locally-rooted, warm, educational voice is differentiated the moment Jordan speaks in the first three seconds of a video.
- **Pediatric and prenatal content is a completely open lane locally.** Despite 

the franchise claiming to serve patients "no matter if you are 2 days old or 90 years old,"

 no pediatric or prenatal content was confirmed from this account. This is the most valuable content category in our practice and our competitor has completely abandoned it.
- **Supplement promotion is a trust liability.** Pushing 

supplement product lines

 as a recurring content pillar signals commerce over care. Our content positioning around education and empowerment reads as more trustworthy by contrast.
- **Facebook is dead for them and can be ignored as a competitive threat.** 

10 people talking about their Facebook page

 means their community is not being activated there. We should watch for any future change but do not need to match their Facebook volume.
- **Their TikTok hook is generic and easy to beat.** "Your spine deserves better" is passive and brand-first. A hook like "Here's what I look for when I adjust a 3-week-old baby" would outperform it without any production budget.
- **They name doctors in the bio but never put them on screen.** Naming Dr. Alban Sims and Dr. Cal Criswell in the bio without ever showing them on camera is wasted credibility. Jordan on camera closes the loop.
- **They post 3-4x per week and have not grown meaningfully.** Frequency alone does not build an audience. Our content strategy should prioritize one excellent video over four forgettable graphics.
- **No local community identity.** There is nothing in any search result tying this account to Woodstock schools, youth sports, local families, or community events. Local rootedness is a moat they have not built and we can.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **Pediatric and infant care content.** Completely absent from their confirmed presence. A short-form series on what newborn adjustments look like and why parents seek them would own a content category this competitor has entirely abandoned locally.
- **Prenatal chiropractic content.** Nothing found on pregnancy wellness, pelvic alignment, or postpartum recovery. This is a high-search, high-share content category and is wide open in this market.
- **Doctor-on-camera educational video.** Zero confirmed instances of either doctor speaking to the camera. Our Format A and D content with Jordan in frame is already differentiated before a word is said.
- **Real patient stories.** No Format G (Patient Story) content was found. Authentic, local testimonials, especially from families or prenatal patients, would create social proof they have no version of.
- **Myth-busting content.** No Format D content found. "Myth: Adjustments aren't safe for babies. Truth: Here's what the research says" is exactly the kind of content that earns shares and saves and this competitor is not doing any of it.
- **Community-embedded content.** No local business tags, school references, youth sports, or Woodstock event tie-ins found. A simple "here's what we're doing this Saturday at [local event]" builds local identity they have never established.
- **Exercise and demo content.** No Format C content was confirmed. A "do this 3 times a day if you sit at a desk" quick-hit demo series would serve Woodstock's working-adult population with something genuinely useful that their competitor does not offer.
- **Humor and personality.** 

People like to follow people, not brands.

 This account has no personality. Any moment of authentic humor or warmth from Jordan is a differentiator that a corporate franchise template cannot replicate.
