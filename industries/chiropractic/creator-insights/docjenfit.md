# Creator Analysis: @docjenfit

**Platform:** Instagram (primary), with cross-presence on YouTube, TikTok, and Podcast
**Followers:** 693K Instagram | 3,234+ posts
**Niche:** Physical therapy, mobility, pelvic floor health, prenatal and postpartum movement, whole-body pain relief
**Analyzed:** 2025-03-05

---

## Overview

Dr. Jen Fraboni, PT, DPT is the creator of @jen.health and co-host of The Optimal Body Podcast, positioning herself around eliminating pain and optimizing movement. She is the mastermind behind two rehabilitation programs, the Mobility Method and the Optimal Body, and has evolved from Instagram educator into a full DTC health platform. Over the years she has built an audience with more than half a million followers through viral videos helping people move out of pain and back into their bodies, making her the clearest parallel to Collective Family Chiropractic in the PT-adjacent education space.

## Top Performing Content

Based on her documented content themes and engagement patterns:

**1. Pelvic Floor Myth-Busting**
Hook type: "Is [common belief] actually true?" / Contrarian Statement Hook
She regularly debunks the usefulness of Kegels and outlines more effective pelvic floor and mobility exercises. These myth-busting frames consistently drive comments and shares from audiences who feel "finally someone said it."
Format: **Format A** (talking head, direct camera address with text overlay)

**2. Posture and Pregnancy Reels**
Hook type: "What you were told about [X] is wrong"
Dr. Jen discusses the significance of mobility, the fallacy of the "perfect posture," and how varying positions can benefit individuals, especially during pregnancy and postpartum, addressing myths related to movement and posture and advocating for tailored fitness and mobility programs.
Format: **Format A/B** (talking head + demo combo)

**3. Pelvic Floor and Breathing Connection**
Hook type: "What happens when you [action]?"
She teaches that the standing diaphragm vacuum creates a tensional pull on all the diaphragm's myofascial connections from the pelvic floor all the way through the face, and that the inability to expand the ribcage to its maximal potential can limit diaphragm motion, thoracic mobility, and even shoulder mobility. Videos showing the breathing-pelvic floor link reliably generate saves.
Format: **Format E** (demonstration with instructional overlay)

**4. "Are Your Hip Flexors Really to Blame?" Style Reels**
Hook type: Contrarian/Challenge Hook
Documented content titles include "Are your hip flexors really always to blame?" and "Does your back flare up during or after dishes?" These everyday-symptom hooks are among her most shareable formats.
Format: **Format A** (direct address, problem-first)

**5. Postpartum Return to Strength Training**
Hook type: Permission-giving / "Here's what no one told you" hook
She covers how postpartum women can safely return to strength training while breastfeeding, sharing practical tips on nutrition, hydration, and gradual exercise progression, emphasizing core and pelvic floor stability in early postpartum stages.
Format: **Format B** (educational narrative with on-screen text)

**6. Scary Diagnosis Demystification**
Hook type: Statistic + Fear-Relief Arc
She and Dr. Dom discuss how medical diagnoses like "degenerative disc disease" and "chondromalacia patella" can be misleading and unnecessarily alarming, explaining that imaging often shows abnormalities in people without pain, and encouraging listeners to focus on pain management and functional improvement through progressive exercise rather than labels.
Format: **Format D** (rapid-fire talking head)

## Content Patterns

- **Myth-first structure:** Opens by identifying a widespread belief, then dismantles it with clinical authority and accessible language. Near-universal in her best content.
- **Body system linking:** Consistently connects body parts that audiences don't expect to be related (pelvic floor + breathing, hip flexors + low back, foot health + knee pain). Creates "I never knew that" saves.
- **Personal experience woven in:** She shares personal experiences including her own pregnancy and the nuances of preparing for a vaginal birth after Cesarean (VBAC), which makes clinical information feel earned rather than lecturing.
- **Empowerment framing:** She frames her practice around "you are NOT your diagnosis, you are NOT your MRI, most importantly you are UNIQUE," consistently shifting the audience from fear to agency.
- **Condition + everyday trigger pairing:** Ties pain symptoms to relatable daily moments (washing dishes, sitting at a desk, sleeping position) to maximize audience identification.
- **Cross-ecosystem funneling:** Every Reel feeds to podcast, podcast feeds to app membership. Content acts as a top-of-funnel for a full product ecosystem.

---

## Hook Preferences

- **Contrarian/Myth-busting hooks** ("It's not just about doing Kegels," "Your hip flexors aren't always to blame") — her signature move, drives comments and shares
- **Symptom-recognition hooks** ("Does your back flare up during or after dishes?") — meets audience mid-problem, extremely high save rate
- **Permission-giving hooks** — especially for pregnancy/postpartum audiences afraid they'll hurt themselves
- **Question hooks aimed at common misdiagnoses** — "Is your diagnosis actually telling you the truth?" style openers that create curiosity gaps
- **Audience-identity hooks** — "If you're pregnant and dealing with pelvic pain..." targets a highly motivated, searching audience
- **Debunk + replace structure** — never just tears down the myth, always offers the better alternative immediately

---

## Format Preferences

- **Format A (Talking Head, Direct Address):** Primary format. Clean background, direct camera, text overlays. Used for myth-busting, permission-giving, and education drops.
- **Format B (Talking Head + Demo Hybrid):** Used when a physical movement demo is needed to make the point land. Common in pelvic floor and breathing content.
- **Format E (Demonstration-Led):** Mobility routines, movement assessments, exercise corrections. All her videos are short, to the point, and easy to follow.
- **Carousel Posts:** Used for multi-step educational content and exercise sequences where saving is the primary CTA.
- **No heavy production:** Relies on clean lighting, stable framing, and on-screen text rather than editing complexity. Keeps volume high without production bottleneck.

## Key Takeaways

- **Clinical credibility + approachable language is her moat.** Her content is laid out in a very customer-friendly way with great illustrations, and she is able to take some highly relevant information and break it down into lay terms, not just the old stuff from 20 years ago. This is exactly what Collective Family Chiropractic should be doing with spinal and nervous system education.
- **Myth-busting is a save and share machine.** Every piece of widely-accepted bad advice in chiropractic (crack your own back, sit up straight, bedrest for back pain) is a pre-loaded hook waiting to be used.
- **The body-system connection angle is underused in chiropractic content.** Her "pelvic floor connects to your diaphragm connects to your jaw" content style translates directly to "your nervous system connects to your immune system connects to your infant's colic" territory.
- **Personal story embeds clinical content in trust.** Her VBAC experience makes pregnancy content feel lived, not lectured. Jordan's own family in the practice is an equivalent asset.
- Her expertise in mobility enhancement and pain management consistently delivers valuable content that appeals to a broad audience including students, doctors, trainers, and other professionals in the movement therapy field — proof that going deep on clinical topics does not shrink your audience, it grows it.
- **Platform-to-product funnel is worth studying.** Jen Health is positioned as a personal gateway to enhanced mobility, reduced pain, and increased strength, guided by experienced doctors of physical therapy. Her Reels are not content for content's sake. Each one has a downstream revenue path.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **Zero family unit content.** Her entire platform focuses on the individual, typically an adult woman. She has no content serving the full family across life stages. Collective Family Chiropractic owns that lane by default.
- **No pediatric or infant content.** Pelvic floor PT for postpartum moms is her strength, but what happens to that baby after birth? Infant nervous system development, torticollis, colic, and feeding mechanics are completely uncovered in her world. This is Collective's direct adjacency play.
- **No dad-facing content.** Her audience skews heavily female. Dad-relatable content (desk posture, sports recovery, carrying kids) is wide open.
- **Chiropractic-specific education is untouched.** She covers PT territory. The "why chiropractic adjustments work" education, the nervous system connection, and the full-body cascade of spinal health are not her domain. These are ours.
- **Grandparent and senior audience completely missing.** Aging well, staying active with grandkids, bone density, and fall prevention are topics she doesn't touch that our practice serves daily.
- **Local and in-office human moments.** Her content is entirely digital and remote in tone. The in-office patient moment, the before/after range of motion, the real family coming in together — that authentic local texture is a differentiator we have that she cannot replicate.
