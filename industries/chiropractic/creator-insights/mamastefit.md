## Overview



MamasteFit is run by Gina (MS) and Roxanne (CNM) and describes itself as a "Midwife, Doula, Pre/Postnatal Trainer" account, also identifying as the author of *Training for Two*, with a focus on pelvic mechanics, birth education, and prenatal/postnatal fitness.

 

The duo are sisters: Gina is a birth doula, founder, perinatal fitness trainer, and Head Coach of MamasteFit, while Roxanne is a labor and delivery nurse and certified nurse midwife.

 

MamasteFit started in October 2017 after the birth of Gina's first daughter, when Gina transitioned off of active duty as an Army Officer to become a stay-at-home mom.





MamasteFit supports its audience through pregnancy, birth, and beyond with online courses, fitness programs, and in-person support in Aberdeen, NC.

 Their content strategy is explicitly "free education first, paid products second," with 

a stated goal of giving away large volumes of information across podcast, Instagram, and YouTube because they want this information accessible to everybody, regardless of finances, while consolidating it better within paid courses.

 

Their most advanced paid offering is a Pelvic Mechanics for the Perinatal Professional Course, designed for fitness, birth, and rehab professionals who want to understand and apply pelvic mechanics throughout pregnancy, birth, and postpartum, taught by Gina Conley (MS Exercise Science) and Roxanne Albert (CNM).





Gina and Roxanne focus their personal energy on content creation, supported by a background team that handles video editing and site management, as well as their husbands and nearby family for childcare.

 

On YouTube, they publish one new workout video and 2-3 educational videos per week.

 The account is one of the most credentialed and clinically grounded prenatal fitness brands on Instagram, with crossover into the birth worker professional community, a podcast, and a brick-and-mortar gym.

## Content Patterns

- **Pelvic floor mechanics is the editorial spine.** Nearly every content category, from prenatal fitness to birth prep to postpartum recovery, is connected back to pelvic floor function. 

Topics consistently covered include managing pelvic pain during pregnancy, returning to fitness postpartum, and handling core and pelvic floor dysfunction.



- **Myth-busting is a primary engagement driver.** The account consistently opens with a common misconception before offering the corrected view. 

Example: "When most people think about pelvic floor health, they think about two things: Kegels and deep squats. But the pelvic floor is far more complex than simply..."

 and 

podcast content explicitly notes: "Spoiler alert, doing kegels are not included in our recommended birth preparation exercises."



- **Symptom-first hooks pull in search-ready audiences.** Content is consistently titled around what a pregnant or postpartum person is Googling, not around anatomy. Examples: tailbone pain, curb walking, pelvic girdle pain, "second night syndrome."

- **The duo's personal birth stories are embedded in content as credibility anchors.** 

Gina's own first birth, where she did not feel supported by her midwife and nurse, directly affected how MamasteFit was created and informed its educational mission.



- **Cross-platform content recycling is systematic.** Blog posts, podcast episodes, YouTube videos, and Instagram Reels all cover the same topics with format-appropriate depth. 

Instagram captions often reference "our latest blog post" for deeper breakdowns.



- **Professional credentialing is embedded in every post.** Gina signs as "MS" and Roxanne as "CNM" in all handles and bylines, signaling clinical authority without being cold or academic.

- **Sunday Q&A is a recurring community touchpoint.** 

Followers are regularly invited to participate in Sunday Q&A sessions on Instagram,

 creating a predictable, save-worthy content ritual.

- **Free resources are used as email/conversion funnels.** 

Free guides include an early postpartum recovery course, postpartum sample workout, prenatal sample, pelvic floor birth prep guide, labor guide, birth partner guide, and birth plan template,

 all traffic-driving from social content.

## Hook Preferences

- **Problem/symptom naming as the lead:** Jumps directly to a named discomfort or fear the audience is already experiencing. Example: "Tailbone pain? Try these!" No preamble, no credential intro, just the problem.

- **Counter-intuitive statement hook:** Opens by stating what most people believe, then immediately subverting it. Example: 

"When most people think about the pelvic floor, they think about strengthening, doing Kegels and squeezing more. But the truth is, a healthy pelvic floor isn't..."



- **Action/demo in motion as the visual hook:** Content like curb walking leads with the physical movement itself. 

The hook is embedded in the action: "Curb walking can be helpful during labor to help baby engage and rotate in the pelvis!"



- **Empathy-first narrative hook:** Opens with the emotional reality before offering education. Example from "second night syndrome" content: reassurance before explanation, normalizing the experience before solving it.

- **Question-as-hook for curiosity gap:** 

Example: "How do we know you're good to leave your postpartum bubble???"

 Three question marks signal conversational, not clinical, tone.

- **Metaphor hook for complex physiology:** 

Example: "Our labor hormones are working together and doing a dance to keep labor progressing."

 Makes clinical information relatable and visual without dumbing it down.

- **Personal story hook:** Using Gina or Roxanne's own pregnancies and births as the opening frame before broadening to educational content. [Inferred from podcast pattern, but consistent with Instagram captions found.]

## Format Preferences

- **Format A (Explainer) is the dominant format.** The majority of identified content explains a single physiological concept (labor hormones, pelvic floor quadrants, "second night syndrome") in 30-60 seconds or a short caption. Educational authority is the primary value offer.

- **Format C (Demo) is the highest-engagement format confirmed.** 

The curb walking demo hit 36.6K likes,

 the tailbone pain exercise demo was cross-posted across platforms, and workout modification demos appear throughout YouTube and Instagram.

- **Format D (Myth Buster) is a recurring structural pattern.** Content is regularly built around correcting a widespread misconception (Kegels as the only pelvic floor exercise, Kegels as birth prep, lying still as rest during pregnancy). This maps naturally to short-form Reels.

- **Format B (Checklist) is embedded within other formats.** Posts like the tailbone pain video ("here are four exercises to try") and Q&A sessions naturally produce list-style, save-worthy formats even when not labeled as checklists.

- **Long-form (YouTube + Podcast) are used for depth, not discovery.** 

YouTube publishes one workout video and 2-3 educational videos per week,

 suggesting it serves the warm, engaged audience rather than cold discovery, which is left to Instagram and TikTok.

## Key Takeaways

- **The "symptom-first, solution-second" hook structure is directly transferable.** Instead of opening with anatomy, open with what the patient is already feeling ("tailbone pain," "hip pain in the third trimester," "neck pain after nursing"). We can use this exact structure for prenatal and postpartum chiropractic content.

- **Pelvic floor content performs for this audience.** MamasteFit's viral content is consistently about pelvic mechanics. As chiropractors, we have genuine expertise in how the sacrum, SI joints, and pelvis connect to pelvic floor function, and that angle is underexplored in chiropractic content.

- **Myth-busting drives engagement in this niche.** The "everyone thinks X, but actually..." structure is a proven hook for the prenatal/postpartum audience. We can apply this to chiropractic myths: "You can't get adjusted while pregnant" or "Babies don't need chiropractic."

- **Sunday Q&A is a repeatable, low-production content ritual.** A weekly "you asked, we answered" format builds community and generates endless content from audience questions. Low barrier to start.

- **Free guides function as conversion tools.** 

MamasteFit deploys free guides (birth prep, pelvic floor prep, postpartum recovery) as lead magnets from social content.

 We can create similar freebie PDFs (e.g., "Chiropractic During Pregnancy: What to Expect" or "5 Signs Your Newborn Needs a Spinal Check") to convert viewers.

- **Cross-platform content recycling extends shelf life.** MamasteFit systematically converts every topic into blog, podcast, Reel, and YouTube. We should build a similar waterfall: one topic, multiple formats, multiple platforms, one week of content.

- **Professional credential display is consistent and low-key.** "Gina, MS" and "Roxanne, CNM" appear in every handle and caption without being overstated. We should consistently note "Dr. [Name], DC" in our handle and sign-offs, not just in bio.

- **The postpartum window is an identified gap even for MamasteFit.** 

Gina has stated that MamasteFit is "definitely really heavy on the prenatal and birth side, and a little bit more lacking in the postpartum aspect of things."

 This is our opening to own postpartum chiropractic content more aggressively.

- **Audience is an active fitness audience, not a passive patient audience.** MamasteFit attracts women who seek information, make empowered decisions, and pay for education. This is our ideal prenatal/postpartum patient type. Content that feels educational and empowering (not "come see us") will resonate.

- **Birth worker and professional crossover is a credibility multiplier.** 

MamasteFit lists Sandhills Family Chiropractic as a partner on their "About Us" page,

 demonstrating that chiropractic is already in this community's referral ecosystem. Collaborating with local birth workers, doulas, and midwives expands reach into this exact audience.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **No chiropractic angle whatsoever.** MamasteFit covers pelvic mechanics, pelvis mobility, and SI joint function in depth, but never references chiropractic adjustment as part of the solution. We can position Webster Technique, sacral alignment, and prenatal adjustments as the clinical complement to the exercise and education MamasteFit provides.

- **Newborn and infant content is entirely absent from MamasteFit.** Their content stops at "early postpartum recovery." We can own the "what comes next" story: birth trauma, infant torticollis, latch issues, and why newborns benefit from a chiropractic check within days of birth.

- **Dad/partner content is untouched.** MamasteFit focuses exclusively on the pregnant and postpartum woman. We can create content that speaks to birth partners, including how they can support mom, what to watch for in baby, and their own postpartum physical neglect.

- **Pediatric content beyond the newborn phase is a complete gap.** No toddler, school-age, or teen content exists in MamasteFit's footprint. We can own chiropractic for kids at every developmental stage.

- **The "why chiropractic first" conversation is never had.** MamasteFit's audience is already seeking body-based, evidence-informed solutions. Content explaining how chiropractic fits into the prenatal care team (alongside midwives, doulas, pelvic floor PTs) would resonate directly with this community.

- **Seniors and grandparents are fully absent.** MamasteFit is entirely maternal. We can own the "grandma's holding the baby all day" content, as well as age-related spinal health content that the MamasteFit audience's parents might engage with.

- **Postpartum "clearing to return" content is under-served.** 

MamasteFit has self-identified being "a little bit more lacking in the postpartum aspect of things."

 We can fill that void with chiropractic-specific postpartum recovery content: SI joint recovery, diastasis recti and spinal loading, returning to exercise, and nursing posture.

- **Mental health intersections are absent from MamasteFit's content.** Postpartum mood, stress, nervous system regulation, and sleep content are untouched. Chiropractic's role in nervous system function gives us a unique angle here.
