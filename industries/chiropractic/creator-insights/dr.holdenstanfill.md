**Platform:** YouTube (primary), TikTok, Instagram, Threads

**Niche:** ASMR chiropractic adjustments, patient reactions, family moments, before/after transformations
**Analyzed:** 2025-03-09

---

## Overview

Dr. Holden Stanfill is a chiropractor, content creator, and YouTube star known for his dr.holdenstanfill channel, where he has built over 1.2 million subscribers primarily by posting videos of chiropractic adjustments. He began posting in early 2022. He self-brands as the "#1 Most Viral Chiro in St. Louis, Missouri" and has built his following by blending high-sensation ASMR crack content with warm, family-inclusive moments. He is now seen across YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram with over 1M+ followers across all platforms combined.

## Content Patterns

- **ASMR-first production.** Titles like "Chiropractor Cracks Neck & Back - Satisfying Sounds!" emphasize audio satisfaction, leaving viewers "relaxed and maybe even a little jealous." Every video treats the crack sound as the payoff.
- **Superlative and extreme framing.** Titles consistently use words like "STIFFEST," "YEARS of pain," "Top 10," "LOCKED UP," "INSANE" to amplify stakes before the viewer even presses play.
- **Family as recurring cast.** The "Baby Adjusts His Mom" series features his baby RJ as a recurring character "adjusting his mom with precision and care," producing heartwarming, shareable moments.
- **Intern and staff as supporting characters.** Titles like "My Intern Cracks My Wife!" introduce office characters that create an ongoing narrative beyond solo adjustments.
- **Pregnancy content woven in.** Content titled "Pregnancy Adjustments Are A Little Different..." appears in his feed, signaling he connects clinical content to personal life stages.
- **Reaction-forward editing.** Patient reactions appear to be the emotional anchor of each video, not just the crack itself.
- **Cross-platform daily posting.** With 1,392+ Instagram posts and active presence across TikTok and YouTube, the cadence is clearly high-volume and daily.
- **Patient diversity as proof.** He has adjusted many different clients, from TikTok influencers to military veterans, broadening his appeal and feeding collab-driven discovery content.

## Format Preferences

- **Format A (Technique Demo, short-form vertical):** Ring Dinger first-timers, ASMR crack compilations. Pure sensory payoff.
- **Format C (Single Patient Adjustment, character-driven):** Pediatric, elderly, bodybuilder, veteran patients. Character diversity drives repeat viewership.
- **Format D (Vignette/Family Moment):** Baby-as-chiropractor, wife adjustments, intern moments. Lowest clinical weight, highest emotional share rate.
- **Format E (Testimonial + Adjustment combined):** "Years of pain" transformation videos where the patient's before state is established, then resolved on screen.
- **YouTube Long-Form:** He released one of his most popular YouTube Shorts in April 2023, but his 1.2M subscriber base suggests consistent long-form content drives his YouTube authority. YouTube appears to serve as the archive and SEO engine, while TikTok/IG serve discovery.

## Key Takeaways

- **Sound is the product.** Every title, caption, and hashtag treats the crack audio as the primary draw. This is ASMR-driven content first, chiropractic education second. The ratio is roughly 90% entertainment, 10% education, nearly the inverse of what we should aim for.
- **Family characters drive repeat watch.** The "Baby RJ" series and wife/intern content proves that introducing recurring cast members builds audience investment beyond any single adjustment video.
- **Superlatives manufacture stakes.** His titles consistently over-index on extreme framing ("STIFFEST," "ALL TIME," "YEARS of pain"). This works for ASMR-seeking scrollers but creates no lasting educational authority.
- **YouTube is his long-game.** YouTube Shorts act as an "open door" to introduce new people, but long-form YouTube is still "where most serious channels build watch time, trust, and real fans." His 1.2M YouTube subscribers dramatically outpace his TikTok, which confirms this.
- **Patient diversity is a discoverability engine.** Featuring influencers, veterans, bodybuilders, and children broadens the search surface area and creates collab-driven discovery content.
- **He is not educational.** There is virtually no "why this works" content. He shows the crack, not the cause. This is a clear gap we can own.

---

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **No prenatal-specific content series.** He features pregnancy adjustments casually, but there is no dedicated prenatal chiropractic series with trimester-specific framing. We can own "Prenatal Series" content entirely.
- **No infant/newborn adjustment education.** His baby content is the baby performing adjustments as a comedic bit, not actual infant chiropractic care with parent education. We have a genuine opportunity to fill this with warm, trust-building content.
- **No "why" content.** He never explains what subluxations are, why adjustments help infants latch, why pregnancy causes SI joint pain, or what a spinal nerve actually does. We can be the educational layer his audience is Googling after watching his videos.
- **No whole-family narrative.** He shows individuals. We show a family ecosystem: mom, dad, baby, grandparent, all at different stages, all at the same practice. That multi-generational story is untouched.
- **No postpartum content.** Zero visible content targeting the fourth trimester. This is a high-need audience with a strong share impulse.
- **No cold-start educational hooks.** His hooks assume you already want to watch a crack. We can win audiences who arrive with a question ("Why does my baby hate tummy time?") rather than a craving for ASMR.
- **Tone depth.** Reviews of his practice mention feeling like family and receiving warm, thorough care. "Not only is he a great chiropractor, he has been incredible with our kids." But his content does not reflect that warmth. We can build a brand where the on-screen personality matches the in-office experience.
