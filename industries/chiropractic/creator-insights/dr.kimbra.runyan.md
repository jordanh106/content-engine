## Overview

Dr. Kimbra Runyan's Instagram bio reads: "Move pain-free for good. Quick mobility routines that actually work. Evidence-based chiropractic | St. Charles, IL. Join my mobility app." She has built a clean, authoritative brand around the idea that movement is medicine, deliberately positioning against gimmicky chiropractic content. She is a licensed chiropractic physician who believes every person deserves individualized care to reach their highest level of functionality, with a goal of educating patients on the benefits of chiropractic and improving their overall quality of life.

## Top Performing Content

Based on her web presence, media appearances, and published blog content, her strongest content pillars are:

1. **Desk Worker Stretches** — Her stretches for people who work at a desk all day earned her a segment on Good Morning America. This is her highest-visibility media moment and likely her most-shared content category. Format: **B (talking head with demonstration)**.

2. **Neck Cracking Explained** — A blog and video series explaining why your neck cracks and whether it is safe to do yourself. Taps the "Is [common belief] actually true?" question hook pattern. Format: **D (myth-bust)**.

3. **Daily Movement Minimum** — Content built around the message "don't overcomplicate movement and mobility work — pick one move and commit to doing it every day this month for one minute." Minimalist, actionable, saves-optimized. Format: **A (demo reel)**.

4. **Posture + Mind-Body** — Content addressing the mind-body connection, with examples like how constant stress causes muscle tension, pain, and headaches, and how constant physical pain leads to more stress and anxiety. Format: **B or E (educational with text overlay)**.

5. **Root Cause Over Symptoms** — She produces content reinforcing that a holistic approach should be the first approach to healthcare, focusing on the body as a whole rather than just symptoms, and treating the root cause instead of covering them up.

## Content Patterns

- **"Simple over complicated" philosophy.** Her throughline is that mobility should not feel overwhelming. Her mobility app, Goodly Simple, is positioned around cutting through chaos, eliminating guesswork, and delivering simple, effective steps to help people move and feel better.
- **Evidence-anchored claims.** She consistently references research studies, positive results from previous cases, and proven methods as her clinical authority — framing it as "using a well thought out map to make sure we're going the right way."
- **Anti-gimmick positioning.** She explicitly signals "no cookie cutter, 5-minute, gimmicky appointments," which carries directly into her content tone.
- **Whole-body framing.** Her approach accounts for movement patterns, stress, posture, anxiety, fitness routine, diet, and activities of daily life as interconnected factors, giving her variety across content topics without leaving her lane.
- **Myth-busting chiropractor tropes.** Topics like self-cracking, "you need to come 3x a week forever," and neck pain cause vs. symptom are recurring themes across her blog and social.
- **Product ecosystem integration.** Her social content consistently funnels to her mobility app (Goodly Simple) and ChiroUp patient engagement platform, making her content explicitly part of a revenue ladder.

## Hook Preferences

- **Question hooks** — "Why does your [body part] [symptom]?" and "Is [common belief] actually true?" are her primary patterns (see neck cracking, posture content).
- **Empowerment reframe** — Hooks that flip a frustration into an actionable win. Example: "Stop Googling your symptoms" style framing, leading to a simple plan.
- **Relatability opener** — Her blog voice opens with "my goal is to make movement simple and mobility more fun — here's how you can start to move better so you can start to feel better," indicating first-person relatability hooks on video.
- **"Daily movement = less pain"** style short declarative hooks that double as mantras and save-worthy phrases.
- **Soft contrast hook** — Implying "most people do this wrong" without being alarmist. Calm, factual correction style rather than fear-based.

## Format Preferences

- **Format A (Demonstration Reel):** Primary format. Quick mobility routines shown on-body, minimal setup, clinic or neutral background.
- **Format B (Talking Head + Demo):** Used for educational explanations — myth-busting, explaining mechanisms (why your neck cracks, how adjustments work), GMA-style desk stretch content.
- **Format D (Text-on-screen / Myth Bust):** Used for belief-correction content (cracking your own neck, "I need to come forever").
- **Format E (Overlay/Educational Slide):** Likely used for tips lists (3 stretches, top posture fixes) given her audience skews toward save-worthy content.
- Minimal use of trending audio. Content relies on credibility and utility over sound trends, keeping it shelf-stable and evergreen.

## Key Takeaways

- **"Simple and actionable" is a proven audience acquisition strategy.** Her bio promise — quick routines that actually work — does the same job a compelling hook does. It sets expectations and attracts the right viewer before the video even plays.
- **Evidence-based positioning is a differentiator, not a limitation.** She has built 124K+ followers without viral adjustment clips or shock-value content. Proof that authority + utility outperforms entertainment in this niche.
- **The app funnel changes how content functions.** Every piece of content she posts is technically a top-of-funnel asset for Goodly Simple. This is a model worth studying: content as acquisition, not just awareness.
- **Destigmatizing chiropractic through normalizing movement works.** Her content rarely leads with "come see a chiropractor." It leads with "here's how to feel better," making chiropractic the natural next step.
- **She uses ChiroUp to drive patient engagement and ensure patients fully understand their treatment plan, exercises, and recommendations — delivered through a user-friendly app so they are never without their exercises.** This is a patient retention and education model that translates directly to content loyalty online.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **Zero family or pediatric content.** Dr. Runyan's entire audience is adults (desk workers, active adults, people in pain). She has no presence in prenatal, postpartum, infant, or pediatric chiropractic. This is Collective Family's entire moat.
- **No "why" content for the nervous system.** She explains mechanics well but rarely connects chiropractic to nervous system development, sleep, colic, or immune function — the pillars that matter to the parents we serve.
- **No life-stage framing.** She does not speak to how movement needs change from pregnancy to postpartum to toddler years to school-age to teenage athletics. That full family arc is wide open.
- **No emotional storytelling.** Her tone is calm and authoritative but not warm or character-driven. Collective Family's Jordan has personality and humor as a genuine differentiator.
- **No community-building content.** Her content is instructional, not relational. There is an opportunity to own the "your family's chiropractor who actually talks to you like a human" lane.
- **No dad, grandparent, or multi-generational content.** The whole-family visit is invisible in her content. That's a unique visual and emotional content opportunity for Collective Family.
