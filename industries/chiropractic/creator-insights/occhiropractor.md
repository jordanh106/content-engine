## Overview

Dr. Alex VanDerschelden is the #1 chiropractic creator on TikTok by follower count, operating out of Newport Beach, CA, under the handle @occhiropractor. His dominance is built on a single repeatable format: the dramatic first-time or long-suffering patient who experiences a visceral, highly audible adjustment and reacts with visible euphoria or disbelief. His content is the chiropractic equivalent of a pop song — short, emotionally satisfying, and instantly shareable regardless of whether the viewer has ever visited a chiropractor.

He has trademarked his own technique brand ("The Magic Hug," an occipital lift) and developed celebrity adjacency (Floyd Mayweather, verified athletes, influencers) that elevates every ordinary patient video by association. Patients report discovering him on social media, watching his adjustment videos, and driving 1.5+ hours to book appointments — a patient pipeline created almost entirely by short-form video.

## What Makes People Drive 1.5 Hours to See Him

These are the specific content and storytelling elements, confirmed by patient testimonials and video analysis, that convert viewers into patients willing to travel:

**1. The adjustment is visually undeniable.**
His videos show the crack happening, the patient's face going from tension to release, and the immediate audible evidence of something working. There is no ambiguity. Viewers see the result before they ever book. The before/during/after arc is compressed into 15-30 seconds, removing all the uncertainty that normally blocks people from booking a first appointment.

**2. The "first-timer" format proxies the viewer's own experience.**
When a nurse who has never been adjusted goes viral, viewers who have also never been adjusted see themselves in her. The occupation ("nurse" — someone who understands health) validates that this is legitimate. The relief reaction gives viewers permission to want the same thing. This format is a decision-trigger disguised as entertainment.

**3. He brands his techniques with proprietary names.**
"The Magic Hug" is not an industry term — it is his term. When someone watches five Magic Hug videos and feels ready to book, they search for Dr. Alex specifically, not "chiropractor near me." Branded technique vocabulary creates destination demand, not commodity demand.

**4. Celebrity patients validate the decision for skeptics.**
When Floyd Mayweather gets adjusted and cracks "like a glowstick," viewers who are skeptical of chiropractic see that a world-class athlete trusted the process. Celebrity adjacency neutralizes the objection "is this real medicine?" before it can be voiced.

**5. Short-form volume creates familiarity and trust before the first appointment.**
Patients report discovering him on social media and watching multiple videos before booking. Daily posting means that a viewer who finds him on Monday has seen 7+ videos by the following Monday. Parasocial familiarity with the doctor — his humor, his hands, his bedside manner — removes the fear of going to a stranger. By the time they walk in the door, they feel like they know him.

## Strengths (Learn From)

**1. Technique branding creates exclusive demand.**
The Magic Hug and Y-Strap are not his inventions, but they are his branded language. Patients specifically request "the magic hug." This is something any practice can replicate by naming what they already do.

**2. Caption = micro-hook, video = full hook.**
His captions function as a second hook layer — the text on screen before you decide to watch. "Coming in from Boston. Had a lot of pressure for me to deliver!" is both a teaser and a promise. He never buries his hook in paragraph two.

**3. Patient selection for content is intentional.**
His most viral videos (nurse, race car driver, Floyd Mayweather, long-suffering patients with years of pain) are not random. He selects patients with characteristics that maximize identification: occupations with broad social recognition, extremes of suffering that create tension, and celebrity names that provide discovery-layer juice. The content begins at the moment of patient intake, not at the treatment table.

**4. Reaction capture is his primary skill.**
The audio-visual payoff in his videos is the patient's face and vocalization during or immediately after the adjustment. He films faces, not just spines. This is a production choice that produces the emotional content that gets shared. A satisfying crack with no face is ASMR. A satisfying crack with a human face processing relief is a story.

**5. Humor is never forced — it is captured, not scripted.**
His comedy moments (the flatulence video, "I think he saw the light," "He cracks like a glowstick") emerge from real interactions. He captions the absurdity rather than manufacturing it. This keeps humor authentic and shareable without making the content feel cheap.

**6. The care framework is emotionally clear even without explanation.**
He never explains the science of chiropractic in his viral content. Instead, he shows a human being in pain becoming a human being in relief. The explanation is implicit. Viewers do not need a nervous system lecture — they need to feel the before and the after.

**7. Social proof lives in the comment section, not the caption.**
His captions do not say "best chiropractor in Newport Beach." His patient reactions and comment sections do. He lets the evidence speak. This is a trust architecture that feels earned, not marketed.

## What We Can Adapt (At Our Scale)

These are formats and techniques a small family practice in Woodstock, GA can execute with one phone camera, no production budget, and authentic patient relationships. This excludes anything dependent on his specific celebrity patient network, Newport Beach market, or production infrastructure.

**Adapt immediately:**

- **First-timer format:** Film first-ever adjustments with permission. The narrative structure is simple: who they are, how long they've been dealing with it, and the moment of release. Any practice has first-timers daily.
- **Occupation hook:** When patients have occupations with high identification value (nurse, teacher, firefighter, coach, stay-at-home mom, youth athlete), lead with that in the caption. "Teacher with 10 years of neck pain finally gets adjusted."
- **Duration-of-suffering hook:** "6 months," "2 years," "since her last pregnancy" — these time markers in captions create tension before the video even plays.
- **Named technique vocabulary:** Name what you already do. Whatever your signature move is, give it a consistent branded label across all content.
- **Reaction capture:** Film the patient's face during the moment of adjustment, not just the technique from behind. The face is the content.
- **Short empathy captions:** "Back pain can take such a toll on you" (his caption, 3.1M views) requires zero production budget. Emotional observation + implicit invitation is the formula.
- **Comedy from real moments:** Don't script humor. Caption it when it happens.

**Do not try to replicate:**

- Celebrity patient content (not available to us at this scale — focus on community figures instead: coaches, teachers, local athletes)
- High-production B-roll or cinematic cuts (his content is raw iPhone-quality footage — this is not the differentiator)
- Newport Beach aesthetic or patient demographic (our differentiator is family and pediatric — lean into what he cannot offer)
