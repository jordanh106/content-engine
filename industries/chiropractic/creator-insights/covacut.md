## Overview



Cole (@covacut) is an artist and creator documenting the creative technology revolution for 300K+ people online, collaborating with brands like Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Adobe.

 

The covacut Instagram channel started from a dorm room, with the goal of making cinematic videos about creative technology, and has since reached over 80 million people. Cole blends interests in storytelling, visuals, and tech.

 

On Substack, Cole is identified as a "creative technologist," and the newsletter is called "hyphen."





Cole grew up in Hong Kong as a queer kid who found community online, which led to starting POP, an LGBTQ+ clothing brand. Cole was originally on the path toward medicine but was deeply unhappy, and while applying for medical school, pivoted toward a creative and technical path.

 

Key influences include cyberpunk cinema (Ex Machina, Arcane, Blade Runner) and Hong Kong noir, aesthetics that serve as a constant reminder that technology carries weight and can both liberate and oppress. Cole references these aesthetics heavily while designing the stories told on-screen.





The Substack newsletter "hyphen" is described as "notes for creative-techies living on both sides of the hyphen," a publication with thousands of subscribers.

 

Cova (Cole's on-screen persona) is known for racking up millions of views on Instagram Reels through a signature AI-enhanced visual style, and has worked with brands including NVIDIA and Adobe.

 The content strategy spans multiple formats: short-form cinematic reels as the flagship product, long-form workflow walkthroughs for the deeper audience, and philosophical writing on Substack for the most committed followers. It is a deliberately slow-creation, high-production model built around craft over frequency.

## Key Takeaways

- **Quality over quantity wins at scale.** 

Content has reached over 80 million people

 on a slow-creation model. For Collective Family Chiropractic, this is validation that 1-2 well-crafted videos per week outperform daily low-effort posts.

- **The workflow IS the content.** 

Showing audiences the actual tools and process in real time creates content that is simultaneously educational and entertaining.

 We can adapt this by showing what a chiropractic adjustment actually involves, step by step, on camera, for different life stages.

- **Lead with philosophy, resolve with practicality.** Cole opens with a values question before delivering the how-to. We can open a video with "What does it actually mean to grow up in a healthy spine?" before delivering a concrete pediatric care tip.

- **Brand partnerships should reinforce identity, not dilute it.** 

Collaborations with Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Adobe

 all serve Cole's creative technologist identity. For us, any partnership (pediatric product brands, prenatal supplement companies, active lifestyle brands) should reinforce our family-first positioning.

- **The origin story is evergreen content.** 

The dorm-room launch and the daily messages from inspired people

 remain active narrative tools. Jordan's origin story, why chiropractic, why families, why this practice, is an always-available hook.

- **Build a depth funnel across platforms.** Reels for reach, X/Threads for real-time conversation, Substack/email for deep believers. We should mirror this: Reels for awareness, email list for patient retention.

- **Cinematic aesthetic stops the scroll before the hook is even read.** Cole's visual quality is itself a hook. Investing in consistent lighting, color grading, and shot composition for our reels would pay compounding dividends.

- **Community-first language drives loyalty.** 

By staying true to what Cole cared about, community became the greatest leverage, and the right people and opportunities followed.

 Our version: speak to what families actually care about, not what clinics traditionally post.

- **"Protect the joy" resonates with an audience that's tired of content that feels transactional.** 

Once creativity feels like commodification, it loses the spark.

 Our content should feel like a service to the family, not a funnel to the appointment book.

- **Self-testing and visible surprise builds trust.** 

Cole being visibly surprised by how accurately AI captures her creative process

 creates authentic credibility. Jordan's genuine reactions to patient outcome stories or anatomy facts have the same trust-building power.

## Gaps (Opportunities for Us)

- **No family or multi-generational content whatsoever.** Cole's entire audience is individual creators and tech workers. The "whole family in one room" angle, newborn to grandparent, is completely unoccupied in this content space.

- **No body-based content.** Cole's world is screens, tools, and minds. The physical human body as a subject, how it moves, grows, heals, and responds to care, is entirely absent and directly in our wheelhouse.

- **No condition-specific storytelling.** Cole never answers "what happens when X goes wrong in the body." Our Format B checklists ("5 signs your toddler's spine needs attention") fill a gap this creator doesn't touch.

- **No prenatal or postpartum representation.** The pregnant and postpartum body is invisible in the creative-tech creator space. We own this lane completely and can build it without competition from creators like Cole.

- **No local or place-based community content.** Cole's content is globally oriented and digital-first. Our physical practice, our specific community, and our actual patients create a warmth and locality that a digital-native creator cannot replicate.

- **No myth-busting in this niche.** Cole does not produce Format D content. For chiropractic, "Myth: cracking your back is bad for you / Truth: here's what's actually happening" is a proven engagement format with zero overlap from this creator's output.

- **No senior or aging well representation.** This life stage is entirely absent from Cole's content. Our seniors audience is an underserved demographic across almost all short-form video creators.

- **Slower production model leaves room for consistent, warm urgency.** Cole's 10-20 hours per video means low posting frequency. We can show up consistently (3-4x/week) with shorter, warmer content that builds parasocial familiarity faster.
