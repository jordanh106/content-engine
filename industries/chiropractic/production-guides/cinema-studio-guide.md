## The Philosophy: Stop Prompting, Start Directing

Cinema Studio 2.0 is not a text-to-video generator. It's a virtual film production suite. The difference matters:

| Text-to-Video | Cinema Studio 2.0 |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Write a prompt, hope for the best | Build a camera rig, choose a lens, direct the shot |
| Random camera behavior | Deterministic camera control (you choose every movement) |
| Inconsistent characters | Lock character appearance across shots |
| One-shot generation | Multi-shot sequencing (up to 6 shots, 12 seconds) |
| No optical physics | Real lens simulation with bokeh, focal compression, flare |
| AI decides the style | You pick the genre, the mood, the pacing |

Higgsfield describes it as: **"The first AI production studio with real optical physics, simulated lenses, and deterministic camera control."**

## The Workflow: Hero Frame First

This is the core principle. Never start with video. Start with a perfect still image.

### Why Hero Frame First Works

When you generate a video directly from a text prompt, the AI has to figure out lighting, composition, character appearance, camera position, AND motion all at once. That's too many variables. Things drift.

When you lock a perfect still image first, the video engine inherits:
- Exact facial geometry and features
- Wardrobe and styling details
- Lighting setup and color temperature
- Environment and set design
- Composition and framing

Then you only need to add motion. One variable instead of six.

### The 4-Phase Workflow

```
Phase 1: RIG BUILDING (Camera Setup)
    Pick your camera body, lens, focal length, aperture

Phase 2: HERO FRAME (Image Generation)
    Generate and perfect your starting image
    Enter the 3D scene to adjust perspective if needed
    Lock it as your reference anchor

Phase 3: MOTION DIRECTION (Video Generation)
    Choose camera movements (stack up to 3)
    Select genre mode for pacing/energy
    Set speed (linear, slow-mo, speed-up, ramp)

Phase 4: MULTI-SHOT ASSEMBLY (If applicable)
    Break into up to 6 shots
    Assign unique prompts, movements, and timing per shot
    Maintain character consistency across all shots
```

Before you generate anything, you configure your virtual camera. This is what makes Cinema Studio feel like a real shoot instead of an AI generator.

| **Sony Venice** | Clean and sharp rendering | Versatile all-purpose |
| **IMAX** | Extremely sharp, strong facial detail | Large-scale, high-impact visuals |

Two categories: **Spherical** (normal capture) and **Anamorphic** (horizontal squeeze creating characteristic flares and wider bokeh).

|------|------|-----------------|----------|

**For your content:** Cooke for character-driven storytelling. Canon K35 for warm, inviting scenes. Panavision C Series for dramatic reveals.

### Focal Length

| Focal Length | Visual Effect | Use Case |
|-------------|--------------|----------|
| **8mm** | Ultra-wide, intense distortion, extreme POV | Stylized action, immersive POV shots |
| **14mm** | Wide with visible environment | Establishing shots, environments |
| **24mm** | Standard cinematic framing, background blur | Most versatile, general use |
| **35mm** | Natural perspective, moderate blur | Dialogue scenes, narrative |
| **50mm** | Strong bokeh, subject separation | Portraits, hero shots, close-ups |

**For your content:** 24mm as your default. 50mm for dramatic close-ups and character moments. 14mm for environment establishing shots.

### Aperture

- **Wide aperture** (low f-stop): Shallow depth of field, blurry background, subject pops. Use for portraits, emotional moments, character focus.
- **Narrow aperture** (high f-stop): Deep focus, everything sharp. Use for environments, establishing shots, landscapes.

## Phase 2: Hero Frame Generation

### Writing Image Prompts

Your prompt should combine these elements:

```
[Subject/character description] + [action/pose] + [environment] +
[lighting/mood] + [camera angle/composition]
```

**Example for a historical scene:**
```
"A determined 18th-century Scottish surgeon in a dimly lit medical study.
He examines a small surgical instrument under candlelight. Leather-bound
books line the stone walls. Warm amber candlelight contrasts against
cold grey stone. Camera positioned at eye level, medium shot.
Cinematic, period-accurate, dramatic."
```

**Example for a wellness/practice scene:**
```
"A modern, warm chiropractic treatment room bathed in golden morning
light. A spine model sits on a clean wooden desk. Teal accent wall
in the background, fresh green plants, inviting atmosphere. Camera
at desk level, shallow depth of field focusing on the spine model.
Clean, premium, professional."
```

### Grid Exploration

Cinema Studio generates images in grid layouts (2x2, 3x3, or 4x4). Use these for rapid iteration:

- **2x2**: Quick first pass to test your prompt concept
- **3x3**: More options to find the perfect composition
- **4x4**: Maximum variety for storyboarding multiple shots

Pick the best frame from the grid as your Hero Frame.

### 3D Scene Access

Once you have a Hero Frame you like, you can **enter the image as a 3D scene**:

- Move within the environment spatially
- Adjust perspective and camera angle
- Refine composition without regenerating
- Explore the scene as if walking through an actual set

This is powerful for finding the perfect camera position before committing to video generation.

### Locking the Reference Anchor

Once your Hero Frame is perfect, **lock it as your reference anchor**. This tells the video engine: "This is exactly what the scene should look like. Inherit everything: faces, wardrobe, lighting, environment." Your video will maintain exact consistency with this locked frame.

## Phase 3: Motion Direction

### Camera Movement Presets (50+)

Cinema Studio offers over 50 camera movement presets. You can **stack up to 3 movements simultaneously** to create complex, layered shots.

#### Core Movements

| Category | Presets | Description |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| **Zoom** | Zoom In, Zoom Out, Crash Zoom In, Crash Zoom Out, Rapid Zoom In, Rapid Zoom Out, Dolly Zoom In, Dolly Zoom Out | Lens-based magnification changes. Crash/Rapid for dramatic emphasis. Dolly Zoom (vertigo effect) for unsettling moments. |
| **Dolly** | Dolly In, Dolly Out, Dolly Left, Dolly Right, Double Dolly, Super Dolly In, Super Dolly Out | Physical camera movement. Dolly vs Zoom: dolly changes perspective (parallax), zoom just magnifies. Super Dolly for exaggerated movement. |

| **Arc** | Arc Left, Arc Right, 360 Orbit | Curved movement around the subject. 360 Orbit for dramatic character reveals. |

### Stacking Movements

You can combine up to 3 movements simultaneously. Examples:

- **Dolly In + Tilt Up + Arc Right** = A rising, approaching, curving shot
- **Crane Up + Pan Left** = A sweeping reveal that rises and turns
- **Zoom In + Focus Change** = Tightening on subject while shifting focus plane

This is how you create shots that feel like a physical camera rig with multiple axes of movement.

### Speed Control

| Mode | Effect | Use Case |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Linear** | Constant speed throughout | Standard smooth movement |
| **Slow Motion** | Slowed movement | Dramatic emphasis, emotional weight |
| **Speed Up** | Accelerated movement | Energy, urgency, montage feel |
| **Speed Ramp** | Changes speed during the shot | Dramatic reveals (slow then fast, or fast then slow) |

## Genre Modes

Genre selection fundamentally changes how the AI handles pacing, motion energy, and camera behavior. It's not just a filter; it affects the physics of how movement unfolds.

| Genre | Motion Character | Best For |
|-------|-----------------|----------|
| **Action** | Fast cuts, energetic camera, dynamic movement | High-energy content, sports, reveals |
| **Horror** | Slow, creeping movement, tension builds | Suspense content, dramatic storytelling |
| **Comedy** | Snappy timing, lighter pacing | Lighthearted content, personality clips |
| **Western** | Wide, sweeping, deliberate movement | Establishing shots, landscapes, drama |
| **Suspense** | Building tension, measured pacing | Story reveals, before/after transitions |
| **Intimate** | Close, gentle, personal movement | Testimonials, emotional moments, wellness |
| **Spectacle (Musical)** | Grand, choreographed, rhythmic | Brand reveals, celebrations, montages |
| **Auto** | AI reasoning engine chooses based on your prompt | When you're unsure or want the AI to match mood |

**For your content:**
- **Intimate** for wellness visualizations, patient journey concepts, calming B-roll
- **Suspense** for storytelling videos like the chainsaw origin story
- **Comedy** for lighter content, personality-driven clips
- **Spectacle** for brand reveal moments, office showcases
- **Auto** for when you want the AI to read your prompt and decide

## Character System

### Character Elements

Upload character images to create "Elements" that maintain visual consistency across scenes:

1. **Upload reference images** of each character (face, wardrobe, styling)
2. **Tag elements in your prompts** to tell the system which character appears where
3. **Lock appearance** so the character looks identical across multiple shots

### Multi-Character Scenes

- Up to **3 characters** per scene
- Assign **emotional states** per character per shot
- Direct who appears in each shot of a multi-shot sequence
- Write **dialogue** explicitly or let the reasoning engine generate it automatically

### Consistency Tips

- **Include faces only in the first shot** of a multi-shot sequence to prevent consistency drift
- Lock character appearance, wardrobe, and environment **before** generating additional scenes
- Use the Hero Frame as your reference anchor for all subsequent shots
- If identity preservation drifts between shots, use that creatively (different angles of the same character can naturally look slightly different)

## Multi-Shot Sequencing

This is where Cinema Studio becomes a real production tool. Instead of generating one clip, you can create an entire sequence.

### Specifications

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Maximum shots per sequence | 6 |
| Duration per shot | 1-12 seconds |
| Maximum total duration | 12 seconds |
| Output resolution | 1080p |
| Character consistency | Maintained across shots |

### Automatic Mode

Let the AI reason about how to break your concept into multiple shots:
- Provide an overall prompt describing the scene or story
- The AI decides shot breaks, camera movements, and pacing
- You can set start and end frames for the full sequence
- Best for: Quick generation, letting the AI surprise you

### Manual Mode

Full directorial control over every shot:
- Assign a unique prompt to each shot
- Define camera movement per shot
- Set duration per shot
- Apply speed ramps per shot
- Place characters explicitly per shot
- Best for: Precise storytelling, planned sequences, professional output

### Manual Multi-Shot Workflow

```
Shot 1: [Prompt] + [Camera: Dolly In] + [Duration: 3s] + [Speed: Slow-mo]
Shot 2: [Prompt] + [Camera: Arc Right] + [Duration: 2s] + [Speed: Linear]
Shot 3: [Prompt] + [Camera: Crane Up] + [Duration: 3s] + [Speed: Ramp]
Shot 4: [Prompt] + [Camera: Zoom In] + [Duration: 2s] + [Speed: Linear]
Shot 5: [Prompt] + [Camera: Static] + [Duration: 1s] + [Speed: Slow-mo]
Shot 6: [Prompt] + [Camera: Dolly Out] + [Duration: 1s] + [Speed: Linear]
                                                    Total: 12s maximum
```

### Limitations

- Start/End frame precision is available in single-shot and automatic multi-shot mode
- Start/End frames are **not available** when using characters in auto/manual multi-shot modes
- Multi-shot maximum is 12 seconds total; for longer content, generate multiple sequences and assemble in CapCut
- Audio can be prompted but may need external refinement for precise sync
- Complex non-verbal character reactions (precise timing of expressions) remain challenging

## Writing Cinematic Prompts

### The Prompt Formula

```
[Shot type & camera angle] + [Subject description & action] +
[Environment & set design] + [Lighting & atmosphere] +
[Emotional tone & pacing] + [Technical style references]
```

### Prompt Techniques

**Layer in cinematic specificity:**
```
BAD:  "A man walks through a dark room"
GOOD: "Medium shot, eye level. A weathered surgeon in a linen coat
       moves cautiously through a candlelit 18th-century study.
       Warm amber firelight casts long shadows across stone walls.
       Leather-bound books visible on shelves. Tense, deliberate
       movement. Cinematic, shallow depth of field, 35mm."
```

**Use temporal progression within prompts:**
```
"For the first two seconds, she stands still, staring at the instrument
on the desk. Then she slowly reaches forward, her fingers trembling
slightly. The candlelight catches her face as she lifts it."
```

**Describe psychological depth, not just actions:**
```
BAD:  "She looks scared"
GOOD: "Her eyes widen in silent recognition, lips pressing together
       as the color drains from her face. She doesn't speak, only
       stares, frozen in the weight of what she's realized."
```

**Reference cinematographers for style:**
```
"Roger Deakins cinematography. Natural light from a single window
casting geometric shadows. Muted earth tones, controlled composition.
The frame feels like a painting."
```

**Specify sensory details beyond visuals:**
```
"The air feels thick and still. Dust particles drift through a single
beam of light. The subject's breath is visible in the cold room."
```

### Prompt Templates for Your Content

**Historical Scene (for storytelling videos):**
```
"[Camera: medium shot, slightly low angle] A [character description]
in a [period-accurate setting]. [Specific action with emotional
weight]. [Lighting description with color temperature]. [Atmospheric
details: texture, particles, ambient elements]. [Pacing note].
Cinematic, [focal length], [film reference or mood]."
```

**Spine/Anatomy Visualization:**
```
"[Camera: macro, slowly orbiting] Detailed 3D visualization of
[specific anatomy]. Soft teal (#0d9488) light illuminating the
structure against a clean dark background. [Specific anatomical
action: alignment, nerve impulse, etc.]. Medical-professional
quality, shallow depth of field, precise and educational."
```

**Abstract Wellness Concept:**
```
"[Camera: slow dolly in] Abstract organic forms representing
[concept: healing, energy, restoration]. [Color palette: teal and
warm gold]. Gentle, flowing movement suggesting vitality and calm.
Particles of light drifting through the frame. Clean, meditative,
cinematic depth of field."
```

**Dramatic Reveal:**
```
"[Camera: crane up, speed ramp from slow to fast] [Subject]
positioned center frame against [dramatic backdrop]. Initial
stillness for 2 seconds, then [dramatic action or reveal].
Lighting shifts from [cool/dark] to [warm/bright] as the reveal
unfolds. [Genre mode: Suspense or Spectacle]. Epic, wide
composition, anamorphic."
```

## Tips for Best Results

### Image Generation

- **Test at low resolution first.** Generate at 480p for iteration, then 1080p for your final version. Saves credits and time.
- **Use grid exploration.** Start with 3x3 grids to find the best composition before committing to a Hero Frame.
- **Enter the 3D scene.** Don't regenerate when the angle is slightly off. Enter the scene, adjust perspective, and save the updated frame.
- **Lock your Hero Frame.** Always lock the reference anchor before generating video. This is the single most important step for consistency.

### Video Generation

- **10 seconds is the sweet spot** for multi-shot sequences. Gives enough room for 3-4 meaningful shots without stretching quality.
- **Don't overstack camera movements.** 2 simultaneous movements look professional. 3 can feel chaotic unless intentional.
- **Match genre to content.** Don't use Action genre for a calm wellness visualization. The pacing will fight your intention.
- **Use speed ramps intentionally.** A slow-to-fast ramp on a reveal shot adds massive impact. Don't apply ramps to every shot.
- **Faces in first shot only** (multi-shot). Including faces only in the first shot prevents consistency drift in subsequent shots.
- **Single conflicting motion = disorientation.** Avoid conflicting camera paths in short sequences. Let each movement serve the story.

### Prompt Writing

- **Be specific about lighting.** "Candlelight" is vague. "Warm amber candlelight from a single source at camera left, casting long shadows on cold grey stone" is cinematic.
- **Reference real cinematographers.** Naming "Roger Deakins" or "Emmanuel Lubezki" in your prompt gives the AI a concrete visual target.
- **Describe what the audience should feel**, not just what they see. "Tense and claustrophobic" directs the AI differently than "small room."
- **Avoid prompt overload.** Focus on one strong concept per generation. Don't describe an entire story in one prompt.
- **Use temporal beats.** "For the first 2 seconds... then..." gives the AI a timeline to follow.

### Assembly in CapCut

- **Color match AI clips to your real footage.** Use CapCut's Adjust panel to match warmth, contrast, and saturation to your Mixed Media preset.
- **Add subtle grain to AI clips.** If your real footage has grain, adding light grain to AI clips helps them blend.
- **Sound bridges.** Carry ambient audio (room tone, music) continuously through AI clips. Silent AI clips feel disjointed.
- **Transition with purpose.** Use simple crossfades (0.5s) between AI clips and real footage. Never use flashy CapCut transitions; they scream "edited" and break immersion.
- **Frame rate consistency.** Export AI clips at 24fps for cinematic feel, matching your real footage shot at 24fps or 30fps (CapCut can adjust).
