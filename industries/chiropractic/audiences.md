## Pregnancy & Postpartum (prenatal) · video codes P1-P9

### Who they are
Women aged 24-38, mostly first or second-time mothers, currently 12-40 weeks pregnant or 0-18 months postpartum. Educated, research-heavy buyers. Active on Instagram and Reddit (`/r/BeyondTheBump`, `/r/BabyBumps`, `/r/Mommit`). Will spend weeks evaluating a practitioner before booking — testimonials and credentials matter more than slick branding.

### What they're worried about
- Pelvic alignment for delivery — "is baby in the right position?"
- Postpartum back pain that won't resolve on its own
- Diastasis recti and how it heals
- Pubic symphysis pain, round ligament pain, sciatica during pregnancy
- The load-bearing question: **"Is chiropractic actually safe during pregnancy?"**
- After birth: feeling like themselves again, returning to exercise safely

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Is chiropractic safe during pregnancy"
- "Webster technique near me"
- "Chiropractor for breech baby"
- "Postpartum back pain how long"
- "Diastasis recti chiropractor"
- "ICPA chiropractor [city]"
- "Pelvic floor and chiropractic"
- "Pubic symphysis pain pregnancy"

### What they save / share
Educational explainers with anatomy visuals. Birth story testimonials from real patients. "What to expect at your first visit" walkthroughs. Postpartum recovery timelines. Trade saves with mom-friends in DMs — share-with-one-friend rate is the dominant signal here.

### What they DM or ask in person
- "I'm 32 weeks, is it too late to start care?"
- "How many sessions before I feel better?"
- "Do you take my insurance / extras?"
- "Are you ICPA / Webster certified?"
- "Can my husband / partner come to the first visit?"
- "Can I still come in if I'm cleared for delivery?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
A patient story from someone with the same condition + life stage (e.g., a third-trimester woman with pubic symphysis pain), followed by a "first visit walkthrough" that demystifies the experience. Anything that reduces uncertainty about safety + insurance.

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Myth opener** — "You probably think chiropractic during pregnancy is risky. The opposite is true."
- **Stat anchor** — "82% of pregnant women experience pelvic misalignment by week 30. Here's what to look for."
- **Story frame** — "Sarah came in at 36 weeks. Baby was breech. Here's what we did."
- **Listicle** — "5 things every pregnant woman should know about her pelvis"

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- Anything that sensationalizes pregnancy ("This will SHOCK you")
- Generic "back pain" content not specific to pregnancy
- Hard-sell CTAs in slide 1 (this audience runs from sales energy)

- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/mamastefit.md`
- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/thrivefamilychirojax.md`

## Babies & Infants (infant) · video codes B1-B10

### Who they are
The mothers from the prenatal segment, now with a baby 0-18 months old. Sleep-deprived, hypervigilant, will-do-anything-to-help energy. Live on Instagram saves and TikTok at 3am. Decision speed is faster post-birth than during pregnancy — when something is wrong (colic, latching, torticollis), they act this week.

### What they're worried about
- Colic and inconsolable crying — "is this normal?"
- Latching / feeding difficulties (often tied to tongue tie or tension)
- Torticollis (head turn preference)
- Flat head / plagiocephaly
- Missing developmental milestones (rolling, sitting, crawling on time)
- Retained primitive reflexes — Moro, ATNR, palmar grasp
- Reflux, gas, sleep regression
- Ear concerns / chronic congestion

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Chiropractor for newborn"
- "Is it safe to adjust a baby"
- "Baby chiropractor colic"
- "Torticollis chiropractor near me"
- "Pediatric chiropractor [city]"
- "Baby tongue tie chiropractor"
- "Flat head syndrome treatment"
- "Webster baby"
- "Retained Moro reflex"

### What they save / share
"What does an infant adjustment look like?" videos (huge fear-reducer). Side-by-side before/after of latch or torticollis. Quick "is this normal?" guides. Pediatric milestone charts. Trade with their NICU / mom-group friends.

### What they DM or ask in person
- "Will it hurt my baby?"
- "How can you adjust someone so small?"
- "How will I know it's working?"
- "Should my baby cry during the adjustment?"
- "How often / how many visits?"
- "Can we still nurse / bottle-feed normally that day?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
A video of an actual infant adjustment showing it's incredibly gentle (typically fingertip pressure), paired with a parent's testimonial about a measurable change (latching improved, sleeping through, less crying). The "will it hurt" fear must be addressed visually, not just verbally.

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Myth opener** — "You probably think adjusting a baby uses force. It's the lightest touch you've ever seen."
- **Show, don't tell** — Demonstration of the actual technique (Format C)
- **Patient story** — "This 6-week-old came in with colic. Here's what we found."
- **Quick tip** — "If your baby always turns their head to one side, watch for this."

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- Anything that mentions "cracking" or "popping" (terrifies this audience)
- Adult chiropractic footage being used to represent infant care
- Stat-heavy intros that don't lead with reassurance

### Signal references
- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/mommaschiro.md`
- Conditions: `colic`, `retained-reflexes`, `moro-reflex`, `feeding`, `torticollis`, `milestones`, `infant-adjustment`, `ear-concerns`, `tummy-time`, `infant-sleep`

## Kids & Teens (kids) · video codes K1-K8

### Who they are
The decision-maker is the parent (usually mom), age 30-48. The kid is 4-17. Parent is balancing school + sports + screens + worry about their kid's posture / focus / growing pains. They're on Instagram for parenting content, Facebook groups for school-district stuff. Will book a kid's appointment fast if they sense a problem — slower if it's "preventive."

### What they're worried about
- Their kid's posture from phones / iPads / school chairs ("tech neck" young)
- Growing pains in legs / hips at night
- Backpack weight + alignment damage
- Focus and attention issues (potentially neuro-developmental, ADHD-adjacent)
- Sports injuries — concussions, sprains, overuse in single-sport kids
- Scoliosis screening (they heard about it from a school nurse)
- Sensory processing differences
- Bedwetting that won't stop

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Chiropractor for kids near me"
- "Kids tech neck"
- "Backpack too heavy posture"
- "Scoliosis screening chiropractor"
- "Growing pains at night"
- "Chiropractor for ADHD"
- "Kid concussion chiropractor"
- "Bedwetting and chiropractic"

### What they save / share
Quick-tip videos parents can implement tonight (backpack adjustment, screen-distance rule). Before/after scoliosis screening visuals. "Signs your kid needs to be checked" listicles. Save-and-show-the-school content.

### What they DM or ask in person
- "Is my kid too young for this?"
- "How early should we start checking posture?"
- "Will it interfere with sports practice tomorrow?"
- "Does insurance cover kids?"
- "Do you do family plans / sibling discounts?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
A short demo showing how to spot a posture issue at home (the parent feels competent + concerned), followed by a clear "what we do at the first visit for kids" walkthrough. Bonus: a teen patient story showing the kid is OK with it (kids can override parents' booking attempts).

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Checklist** — "Signs your kid's posture is silently wrecking their spine"
- **Demo** — "The 30-second backpack check every parent should know"
- **Stat anchor** — "Kids look down at screens an average of 4 hours a day. Here's what that does."
- **Myth buster** — "Growing pains aren't 'normal' — here's what they actually mean."

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- Adult-focused pain content that just swaps in "kid"
- Fear-based "your kid is going to need surgery" content
- Anything that lectures the parent

### Signal references
- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/posturepro.md`
- Conditions: `focus-attention`, `growing-pains`, `tech-posture-kids`, `backpack`, `youth-sports`, `scoliosis`, `bedwetting`, `sensory`

## Active Adults & Athletes (athlete) · video codes A1-A6

### Who they are
Adults 22-45 who lift / run / climb / surf / play team sport seriously. Care about performance and recovery. Will swap pain-tolerance stories at the gym. Watch Huberman / Attia podcasts. On TikTok and YouTube more than Instagram. Quick to book if they hear something will keep them training; quick to skip if it sounds passive or "wellness."

### What they're worried about
- Pain that's interrupting training cycles (low back from deadlifts, shoulder from pressing, hip from running)
- Returning to play after an injury — when is it safe?
- Performance plateau they suspect is structural
- Recovery between sessions (especially after a heavy lifting block or long run week)
- Weekend warriors injuring themselves doing too much on Saturday
- The "desk job all week, athlete on the weekend" mismatch

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Chiropractor for runners"
- "Lower back pain deadlift"
- "Chiropractor for athletes near me"
- "When can I lift again after [injury]"
- "Hip mobility chiropractor"
- "Sciatica running"
- "Post-workout recovery chiropractor"
- "Active release technique near me"

### What they save / share
Specific exercise demos with form cues (Format C). "Pre-lifting mobility flow" 30-second tips. Stat-heavy content about performance gains. "What I wish I knew before [injury]" content. Share with training partners.

### What they DM or ask in person
- "Can I still train tomorrow / today?"
- "Will this work for my [sport]?"
- "How is this different from massage / physio?"
- "Do you understand [sport-specific demand]? Have you treated runners / lifters / climbers before?"
- "How many sessions before I can compete again?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
A patient story from someone in their sport (lifter, runner, climber) showing they got back to performance, not just out of pain. Combine with a specific drill or mobility cue they can try at home — proves competence.

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Demo** — "The 60-second hip opener every runner should do before they lace up"
- **Myth buster** — "You probably think foam rolling fixes your IT band. It doesn't. Here's what does."
- **Stat anchor** — "Your hip flexors are short. Here's the cost — measured in watts."
- **Story frame** — "Mark tore his hamstring 6 weeks ago. He's running again. Here's the plan we used."

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- "Wellness" language ("balance," "harmony," "energy flow")
- Passive recovery framing (this audience wants agency)
- Slow-paced content (athletes scroll fast)

### Signal references
- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/docjenfit.md`
- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/covacut.md`
- Conditions: `sports-injury`, `performance`, `return-to-play`, `weekend-warrior`, `recovery`, `desk-to-gym`

## Adults & Daily Life (adult) · video codes D1-D11

### Who they are
The biggest segment by population. Adults 28-55 with a desk job, kids, a mortgage. Wake up stiff. End the day with a headache. Have tried massage, maybe physio, but pain keeps coming back. On Instagram + Facebook. Time-poor — content has to deliver value in under 30 seconds. Strong scroll-past instinct for anything that sounds like generic "wellness."

### What they're worried about
- Chronic low back pain (especially after long sits)
- Tech neck / headaches that build through the workday
- Sciatica radiating into the leg
- SI joint pain, hip stiffness
- TMJ / jaw tension (often paired with stress)
- Sleep quality — "I wake up more tired than when I went to bed"
- The slow drift toward "this is just how my body is now"

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Chiropractor near me"
- "Low back pain desk job"
- "Tech neck exercises"
- "Sciatica chiropractor"
- "Headaches every day what's wrong"
- "TMJ chiropractor"
- "Why do I wake up stiff"
- "Best office chair for back pain"

### What they save / share
"Do this 30-second thing every morning" content (Format F). Workstation setup checklists. Headache-vs-migraine differentiators. Short demo videos with form cues. Don't share much with friends — save for themselves, return-watch later.

### What they DM or ask in person
- "How long until I feel better?"
- "Will I need to keep coming forever?"
- "Do you take Bupa / Medibank / NIB?"
- "What if I have a herniated disc?"
- "Can I keep working out / playing golf / running?"
- "Are you accepting new patients?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
A morning-stiffness explainer that names exactly what they're feeling (specificity) + a 30-second routine they can try tonight. When that routine works, they book. The bridge is: "this person understands my specific problem AND gave me something useful before asking for anything."

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Quick tip** — "Do this 30-second thing the moment you sit down at your desk"
- **Listicle** — "5 signs your morning stiffness isn't normal"
- **Myth buster** — "Stretching your tight hamstrings is making your low back worse. Here's why."
- **Stat anchor** — "67% of desk workers wake up in pain. The cause isn't the mattress."

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- Generic wellness platitudes
- Long-form intros (they bounce in 2 seconds)
- Content that requires equipment they don't own

- `industries/chiropractic/creator-insights/100chiropractic_woodstock.md`

## Seniors & Aging Well (senior) · video codes S1-S5

### Who they are
Adults 60-85 who refuse to "just accept" stiffness and pain. Either the patient themselves OR (often) their adult child who searches for them. On Facebook primarily — Instagram secondarily. Decision style: cautious, conservative, want credentials and bedside manner more than slick branding. Will refer five friends if treatment works.

### What they're worried about
- Falls — they or their spouse has had a near-miss
- Arthritis pain that's getting in the way of grandkid time
- Mobility loss — "I used to walk to the shops, now it's painful"
- Balance and standing endurance
- "I'm scared of being adjusted at my age"
- Wanting to stay active enough to travel / play with grandkids / golf
- Whether chiropractic is safe with osteoporosis

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Chiropractor for seniors"
- "Is chiropractic safe with osteoporosis"
- "Balance exercises for elderly"
- "Falls prevention chiropractor"
- "Arthritis pain relief without medication"
- "Mobility chiropractor [city]"
- "Geriatric chiropractic"

### What they save / share
Gentle exercise demos. Patient stories from same-age people. "Is it safe at my age?" reassurance content. Share with their spouse and their adult kids (who often co-book).

### What they DM or ask in person
- "Is this safe for someone with osteoporosis?"
- "Do you adjust people my age?"
- "What if I'm afraid of the cracking?"
- "Can my doctor recommend whether this is right for me?"
- "How long has this practice been here?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
A testimonial from a same-age patient who got their mobility back, paired with a gentle "what an adjustment looks like for someone older" walkthrough. The fear is "this person is going to hurt me" — needs to be visually addressed. Mention low-force techniques (Activator, instrument-assisted) by name.

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Patient story** — "Margaret is 73 and walks 3km every morning now. Here's the journey."
- **Myth buster** — "You probably think you're too old to be adjusted. The opposite is true."
- **Quick tip** — "The 2-minute balance check that predicts falls"
- **Demo** — "Gentle mobility flow for stiff hips, no equipment"

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- Anything that uses "old" / "elderly" pejoratively
- High-energy / fast-cuts content
- Hard-sell CTAs (this audience wants to think it over with their family)

### Signal references
- Conditions: `mobility-aging`, `arthritis`, `staying-active`, `falls-prevention`, `never-too-late`

## Whole Family & General (general) · video codes G1-G8

### Who they are
The "front-door" audience. Adults 30-45 looking for a family chiropractor — they're booking the first appointment for themselves, but they want a practice that can treat their partner and kids over time. On all platforms. They will read every Google review, look at the Instagram grid, and decide based on vibe before booking. The brand-fit audience.

### What they're worried about
- Finding a practice that "gets" their whole family's needs
- Whether the chiropractor is qualified for pregnancy + kids + sport, not just adult back pain
- Cost and frequency commitment
- "Is this place for me?" — the cultural fit question
- What an adjustment actually feels like (most have never had one)

### What they search for (verbatim)
- "Family chiropractor [city]"
- "Best chiropractor near me"
- "Chiropractor reviews [city]"
- "Holistic family practice"
- "Chiropractor that treats kids and pregnancy"
- "[Suburb] chiropractor open Saturday"

### What they save / share
Office walkthroughs (Format E). Behind-the-scenes content. Team member intros. Family-vibe content. "Day in the life at a chiro practice." Share with their spouse / partner before booking.

### What they DM or ask in person
- "Do you treat kids / pregnancy / [specialty]?"
- "What does the first visit involve?"
- "How much is the first appointment?"
- "Are you open Saturday?"
- "Can I bring my kids?"

### Conversion trigger `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
The practice's personality on display — an office tour (Format E), a team intro, a real moment of warmth. They're buying the practice's vibe, not the technique. Bonus: a multi-generational story (treating three generations of one family).

### Hook archetypes that work
- **Office tour / walkthrough** (Format E)
- **Team intro / day in the life**
- **Story frame** — "Three generations of the Smith family come here. Here's why."
- **Quick tip** — Useful, brand-friendly content that signals expertise without selling

### Hook archetypes that flop `[REVIEW: Jordan + Ashley]`
- Aggressive new-patient promos
- Clinical-feeling content that hides the warmth
- Content that pretends to be edgy when the practice is family-friendly

## Cross-audience patterns (signal hierarchy)

**Highest-converting signals across ALL audiences:**
1. **Same-life-stage patient stories.** Audiences trust people like them.
2. **Specific anatomical / mechanism explainers.** Education > sales.
3. **First-visit walkthroughs.** Unknowns drive churn at the booking step.
4. **Demos with takeaway value.** Give something before asking for anything.

**Signals that consistently underperform:**
1. Generic wellness language with no specificity.
2. Hard-sell CTAs in slide 1 (does worse than soft saves).
3. Adult content reused for pediatric / pregnancy audiences (loss of trust).
4. Trend-chasing audio / dance content that doesn't match the practice's tone.
